1. Never Have I Ever

Someone admits something they have never done; everyone who has drops a finger. The lowest barrier to entry of any party game: no setup, works with strangers, because the confessions build the shared history everything else relies on. This is the one you open with.

2. Truth or Dare

Needs no introduction, dies without good material. The trick is a room of prompts that actually get used, plus one hard veto each so the pressure stays theatrical rather than mean. Great once the group has warmed up.

→ Rules and 40 prompts   → Free generator

3. Most Likely To

Read a "who is most likely to...", count to three, everyone points. Most fingers wins the round, and the defence speeches are the real content. The best game here for groups with genuine history; save it for your actual mates rather than strangers.

→ Rules and 40 questions   → Free generator

4. Would You Rather

An impossible dilemma, a public vote, and then the interrogation. The simultaneous vote is what makes it a party game rather than a car game: everyone commits at once, then has to defend it. Scales beautifully to big groups.

→ Rules and 32 dilemmas   → Free generator

5. What Are The Odds

The chaos engine. You dare a mate, they name the odds, you both count down from three and shout a number. Match, and it has to happen. No other party game produces a genuine "no way that just happened" moment as reliably.

→ Rules and 50 dares   → Free generator

6. Imposter

The sit-down schemer. Everyone secretly gets the same word except one player, who gets nothing and has to bluff. A few rounds of oblique questions, then a vote. Catch the imposter or get caught. Best from four players up to about ten.

→ How to play Imposter

7. The Four Kings

The one game here with real structure and a finale: a full deck in a ring, every card a rule, and the last card ends the round. It is the party-only take on the classic circle-of-cards game, forfeits in place of anything else.

8. Antics mode

The flagship. One phone deals a mixed stream of dares, rules that stay live for several cards, votes and callouts aimed at players by name, so nobody has to be the host. It is the answer to "what do we play?" when nobody wants to decide.

Party games FAQ

What are the best party games for adults?

For a group of adults, the reliable ones are Never Have I Ever to break the ice, Most Likely To for groups with history, Would You Rather for arguments, What Are The Odds for chaos, and Imposter for a sit-down round. Most nights run two or three back to back, and all eight live in the Antics app on one phone.

What party games need no equipment?

Never Have I Ever, Truth or Dare, Most Likely To, Would You Rather and What Are The Odds need nothing but people and at most one phone to read from. No cards to buy, nothing to print, nothing to carry.

What is the best party game app?

Antics puts eight games and 2,000 cards on one phone with no ads and no accounts. It is party-only, built on forfeits rather than drinking, and free on iPhone and Android.

What are good party games for large groups?

Most Likely To and Would You Rather scale best for big groups because everyone votes at once, and Imposter works up to about ten players. Pass-the-phone games avoid having to buy enough physical sets for everyone.
