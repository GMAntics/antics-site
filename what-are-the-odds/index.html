What Are The Odds: rules + 50 prompts worth the risk

The purest dare mechanic ever invented: one question, one countdown, two numbers. Nobody makes you do anything. You set your own odds, and then the universe decides. Rules in 30 seconds, then 50 prompts, all forfeit-based, none of them filler.

How to play: anyone asks anyone "What are the odds you [do the thing]?" The target sets a range based on pure dread: "1 in 15." Count down together, "three, two, one", and both of you say a number from 1 to 15 at the same time.

Same number: they do it. The prompt is the forfeit. Different numbers: nothing happens, and someone else picks a new victim.

The whole psychology: "1 in 2" is a coin flip, which means you basically fancy it. "1 in 100" means absolutely not. Everything in between is negotiation.

The rules, properly

Setting the odds

The target picks any range from 1 in 2 up to 1 in 100. The number is a dread-o-meter and everyone can read it: 1 in 2 means "honestly, fine", 1 in 5 means "persuadable", 1 in 20 means "I'd rather not", 1 in 100 means "no, and stop asking". House rule worth adopting: cap it at 20, or the night stalls while cowards hide behind big numbers.

The countdown

Both players count "three, two, one" out loud and say their number simultaneously. That word is doing all the work. Hesitating gets a redo. Waiting a beat to hear the other number first counts as an automatic match, no appeal, everyone saw it.

The match

Same number and the target does the thing, right now, not "later" and not "next time we're out" (unless the prompt says so). No match and the round simply evaporates. That's the beauty of it: most rounds are free, which is exactly what makes the hits so devastating.

Refusing

You can refuse a matched dare. It costs you: the group picks a replacement forfeit instead, and group-picked is never gentler than the original. Refusing the replacement too means you've stopped playing the game, which the group will remember for years.

The comeback

Optional but recommended: anyone who completes a dare earns a free "what are the odds" at their asker before play moves on. It keeps the askers honest. Nobody fires a 1-in-3 at you if they know one's coming straight back.

Variations

Group odds

For big rooms: one person asks the whole group, everyone sets a shared range, everyone counts down and says a number against the asker. All matchers do the dare together. Excellent for prompt 29.

The rebound

If the target survives, they can immediately fire the exact same dare and odds back at the asker before anyone else speaks. Ask questions you could survive yourself.

Sudden death

Any argument during the night (whose round it is, whose turn to make tea, who started it) can be settled by 1 in 2, winner's version of events becomes official history.

What Are The Odds FAQ

What are the rules of What Are The Odds?

One player asks another "what are the odds you do X?" The target sets a range, for example 1 in 10. Both count down from three and simultaneously say a number in that range. If the numbers match, the target does the dare. If not, nothing happens and play moves on.

What happens if you say 1 in 2?

It's a coin flip, so saying it is basically consent with a drum roll. Seasoned players use it as a flex: "1 in 2" on a scary dare tells the room you were always going to do it.

Can you refuse a dare after the numbers match?

You can, but the group picks a replacement forfeit instead, and the group has never once picked something gentler. Matching and then doing the thing immediately is always the better story.

What if someone says a number outside the range?

Counts as an instant match. Same for changing your number after hearing theirs. Both are cowardice taxes, and both are enforced with enormous enthusiasm.

Is What Are The Odds a drinking game?

It gets played a hundred ways. This version runs on forfeits: the dare is the whole point, everyone can play, and the prompts stay the star. It also means the game works at a family barbecue, a sleepover or a six-hour airport delay.

Whose turn is it?

There are no turns. Anyone can fire at anyone, whenever. The only rule is one live challenge at a time, because two simultaneous countdowns is how living rooms descend into chaos. Beautiful, beautiful chaos, but still.

Play it right: the odds ARE the consent. The target sets the number, and 1 in 100 is a polite no. Respect the hundred, keep dares in the room or in the group chat, and never aim one at something you know is actually raw. Look after your mates.
