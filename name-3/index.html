Name 3: the five second game

One category, five seconds, three answers. It sounds easy until the clock starts and your brain goes completely blank on pizza toppings. Rules in 30 seconds, then free categories to play with right now.

How to play: whoever's turn it is reads the category out loud, say "Pizza toppings", and starts the clock. They have five seconds to name three things in it, out loud, before time is up.

Three before the buzzer: a point. Fewer than three, or a pause long enough for the room to start laughing, and it's no points. Then the phone goes to the next player.

The whole trick: anyone can name three pizza toppings. Almost nobody can do it while five people stare at them and a clock ticks down.

The rules, properly

The clock

Five seconds, starting the moment the category is read. Not when you feel ready, not after a deep breath. In the Antics app the clock ticks and buzzes, so nobody argues about when time ran out.

What counts

Three different answers that fit the category, said out loud before the buzzer. Saying the same thing twice doesn't count, and neither does a word that only becomes an answer halfway through. The room is the judge, and the room is never fair.

Scoring

One point for every round you get. No point for a round you don't. In the app the player taps Got it or Failed, and a leaderboard behind the trophy keeps the room's standings for the night.

No forfeits

Name 3 runs on points, not forfeits. Failing a round costs you nothing but your dignity, and five seconds of everyone remembering how you forgot what colours are.

Variations

A longer clock

For a room that is new to it, or a few hours into the night, give everyone more time. The app lets the room change the clock from the lobby before it starts.

The steal

If a player fails, the next player gets the same category and the clock again. Get three and you take the point.

Name 5

For anyone who finds three too easy. Same clock, five answers. It humbles people quickly.

Name 3 FAQ

What is the five second game?

A party game where one player gets a category and five seconds to name three things in it. Name 3 is Antics' version: one phone, one category per turn, one point for every round you get.

How many players do you need?

Two is enough, and it gets louder the more of you there are. Type everyone's name into the app and it says whose turn it is and keeps a score for each player.

Who decides if an answer counts?

The room. If most people would accept it, it counts. If you have to explain it, it doesn't.

Can you make the clock longer?

Yes. Five seconds is the classic, but the app lets the room set the clock in the lobby before you start.

Is there a forfeit for failing?

No. Name 3 is scored on points, so a failed round simply earns nothing.
