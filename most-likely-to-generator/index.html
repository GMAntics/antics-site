How to run Most Likely To from this page

  1. Pick a reader. They tap Draw a card and read the line out in full, starting with "Most likely to".
  2. Give everyone a second to look round the room, then count it down together: three, two, one, point.
  3. Every hand goes up at the same moment. Nobody waits to see where the crowd is aiming, and nobody points at the ceiling.
  4. Count the fingers. Whoever collects the most is the answer, and they get one short speech in their own defence.
  5. The phone passes to whoever was picked. They draw next, so the spotlight keeps moving round the group.

Playing for stakes? Give the winner of each round a forfeit; the forfeit ideas page has plenty that need no props. Traditional rules, tie-breaks and a much longer list sit in the Most Likely To guide.

Where Most Likely To works best

Uni flats

A few weeks into sharing a kitchen, everybody knows who leaves pans to soak for days and who has never once bought washing-up liquid. This game turns all of that into an evening's entertainment instead of a flat meeting. More ideas for halls and house shares are on party games for uni.

Work socials

Stick to the clean deck, and save it for a team that has been together a while, because pointing needs shared history. A line like "most likely to be running the place in ten years" hands the quiet ones a moment of glory without putting anybody on the spot.

Weddings

Leave a phone open on the clean deck at each table between courses, or bring it out at the rehearsal dinner so the two sides get to know each other. The best rounds end with an entire table pointing at the best man.

Nights in

Four or five old friends on a sofa are the ideal crowd for the party deck. Everyone has years of evidence, so each round becomes a debate, and the defence speeches are often funnier than the card that started them.

Most Likely To generator: quick answers

Will the same card come up twice?

Not until you have been through the whole deck. The generator shuffles every card when the page loads and deals them in that order. At the end it tells you so, and Start again reshuffles.

Can I send a card to the group chat?

Yes. After each draw a Copy button appears. On most phones it opens the share sheet; on a laptop it copies the line, ready to paste anywhere.

How many people do you need?

Three at the very least, so a vote means something. Five to ten is better: enough fingers for a clear winner, and enough people for the arguments to get going.

Does it need a signal?

Only to open the page. The deck loads with it, so every draw after that happens on your phone and a patchy connection will not stall the round.
