How to play: read a "who's most likely to…" out loud. Count down from three, and everyone points at someone: no abstaining, self-votes allowed if you're honest. Whoever has the most fingers on them takes the forfeit. Split decision? Both take it, then the room debates it.

This guide explains the traditional game as it is played offline. Antics for iOS is a party-only app built on forfeits and contains no drinking instructions.

Why this game works (and when it doesn't)

Most Likely To runs on shared history: the pointing is the punchline, and the defence speeches are the content. It's the best game on this site for a group that's known each other for years, and the weakest for a room of strangers (they've got nothing to point with: give them Never Have I Ever first and come back in an hour).

Most Likely To FAQ

What are the rules of Most Likely To?

Someone reads a "who's most likely to…" question, everyone simultaneously points at a player on the count of three, and whoever collects the most fingers takes the forfeit. No abstaining: pointing is mandatory.

What happens on a tie?

Both take the forfeit. Then the room argues about who deserved it more, which is the game working as intended.

Can you vote for yourself?

Yes: a confident self-point is one of the great moves in the game. It counts like any other finger.

Play it right: read the room: the questions are about affectionate exposure, not actual grievances. Look after your mates.
