What is a pass the phone game?

A pass the phone game is a party game where the phone does the dealing. Whoever is holding it reads the card on screen out loud, the room does what it says, and the phone moves on. There is no board, no pack of cards and no quizmaster, and nobody has to learn the rules first, because every card carries its own.

Antics mode is the pass the phone game at the heart of Antics. It deals five kinds of card in one feed: a question for the whole room, a call-out that names one player, a new rule that sticks for a few cards, a quick challenge, and a vote where everyone points at once. You never know which kind is next, which is the point. If you have played Picolo, the shape will feel familiar (how Antics compares).

How to play

  1. Open Antics and tap the Antics tile.
  2. Add everyone’s names, or skip it. With names, call-outs land on a real person. Without them, the card picks on your left neighbour.
  3. Whoever is holding the phone reads the card out loud.
  4. Do what it says. A forfeit sits under every card for whoever loses.
  5. Tap for the next card. Pass the phone whenever you like.

That is the whole game. When a new rule comes up, it stays at the bottom of the screen with a countdown of how many cards it has left, and the app tells the room when it is over.

The five kinds of card

Here are ten cards from the deck, two of each kind, exactly as Antics deals them. Cards that name a player use whoever you put in the game; here we have made up the names.

EVERYONE A question for the whole room

Anyone it is true for owns it and takes the forfeit.

  1. Take the forfeit if your last text was to someone you fancy.
  2. Take the forfeit if there are more screenshots than photos in your camera roll.

CALL-OUT One player, by name

There is nowhere to hide. Without names in the game, it lands on your left neighbour.

  1. Sam, two truths and a lie. Everyone guesses the lie. Anyone who guesses wrong takes the forfeit.
  2. Priya, guess the last person your left neighbour texted. Wrong = your forfeit, right = theirs.

NEW RULE Rules that stick around

A rule lasts between four and ten cards, depending on the rule, and a countdown on screen shows how many are left. Both of these last six.

  1. New rule: end every sentence with “…allegedly”. Slip = forfeit.
  2. New rule: Jess only talks about themselves in the third person. Slip up, take a forfeit.

CHALLENGE A quick game inside the game

Everyone plays at once. Slowest, or first to crack, pays.

  1. Everyone fake-laugh as hard as possible for five seconds. Anyone who laughs for real after takes the forfeit.
  2. On three, everyone makes their photo face. Hold it. First to laugh takes the forfeit.

VOTE On three, everyone points

The room decides, and pointing at yourself is allowed.

  1. Point at who takes longest to get ready. Most votes takes the forfeit.
  2. Point at the best liar in the room. Most votes takes the forfeit.

What happens when you lose a card

You take a forfeit: something quick and daft, dealt by the app along with the card, and a fresh one comes with every card. The app never tells anyone to drink, so whoever is not drinking tonight plays exactly the same game. Playing something else and need a few? Borrow from our forfeit ideas.

Why it never gets old

Antics remembers which cards your phone has already shown, so the next night deals ones you haven’t seen. There are thousands of cards across the app, and more are added all the time. Skip anything that doesn’t suit the room: it’s one tap and nobody notices.

More pass the phone games in Antics

Antics mode is one of nine games in the app, and most of the rest work the same way: pass the phone, read it out, see what happens.

  • Never Have I Ever: read the confession, and everyone who has done it owns up.
  • Most Likely To: on three, everyone points at who fits the card.
  • Would You Rather: two choices and no dodging. Pick one and defend it.
  • Truth or Dare: the classic, with a pile of each.
  • What Are The Odds: set the odds, count down and shout a number. Match, and the dare happens.
  • Imposter: everyone gets the secret word except one player, who has to bluff.
  • The Four Kings: the circle card game, with a rule for every card.
  • Spicy: the after dark deck, strictly 18+.

Do we have to add names?
