How a round of What Are The Odds works

  1. The reader taps Draw a card, picks a target and asks it to them by name. Every card already opens with "What are the odds you'd", so it comes out as a challenge.
  2. The target sets the odds as a range: 1 in 3 if they are game, 1 in 20 if they would really rather not. Plenty of groups cap it at 20 so nobody hides behind a huge number.
  3. Both of them count down out loud, three, two, one, and say a number inside that range at exactly the same moment. Saying it late counts as a match.
  4. Same number? The dare happens there and then. Different numbers, and the round simply vanishes.
  5. The target becomes the next reader, so whoever was just challenged gets to choose a victim of their own.

Because the target picks the odds, nobody is pushed into a dare they did not gamble on, which makes this the gentlest dare game going for a mixed group. Full rules, the rebound and group odds variants, and more prompts sit on the What Are The Odds guide.

What Are The Odds generator: answered

What odds should I give?

Whatever you honestly feel. The number is a public measure of dread: 1 in 2 says you are basically up for it, 1 in 10 says persuade me, 1 in 20 says please ask somebody else.

Who does the generator dare?

Nobody in particular. The card supplies the dare and the reader chooses who to ask, so spread it round: the same person challenged three times running is not a game, it is a campaign.

Can you back out after a match?

You can, but it costs you. The usual price is a replacement dare chosen by the group, and group choices are rarely kinder. Keep a few from our forfeit ideas page in reserve.

Why does every card begin the same way?

So it can be read straight out. The generator puts "What are the odds you'd" in front of each dare and turns it into a question, which is exactly how the game is asked out loud.
