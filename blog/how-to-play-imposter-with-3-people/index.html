How to play Imposter with 3 people (and still have a game)

Played the normal way, three-player Imposter is over in one vote. Change five small things and it turns into one of the tensest games you can play with just two mates on a sofa.

This post assumes you know the basic game: everyone gets the same secret word except the imposter, clues go round the circle, and everyone points at a suspect on three. If not, the full rules take two minutes. Here is what changes when there are only three of you.

The short answer Yes, Imposter works with three. Deal it on a phone, not paper. Keep clues to one word and play one circuit. Let anyone go first, imposter included. Leave the imposter's hint on, and score every round so being caught is not the end of the imposter's night.

Why three players breaks the normal game

With three, there are two players who know the word and one who doesn't. If the two who know trust each other, they both point at the third person and the vote is over. The imposter's own vote doesn't matter.

So the game changes shape. In a big group, the imposter is trying to blend in. With three, blending in is not enough: the imposter has to make one of the other two doubt their partner. And the two who know the word have a different job too. Their clue does not need to convince the room. It needs to convince one person, without handing the answer to the other.

Fix 1: deal it on a phone, not paper

The paper method needs someone to write the word on the strips. In a big group, the odds of that person drawing the imposter strip are small, or they can sit the round out. With three, nobody can sit out, and there is a one in three chance the writer draws the imposter strip already knowing the answer, which kills the round. Use a phone instead. The free Imposter dealer on this site runs from three players: set the count to three, pass the phone round, and each person taps to see their role.

Fix 2: one word each, one circuit

With three players, every extra clue helps the imposter more than anyone else, because they are piecing the word together from only two other people. Keep every clue to a single word, play one circuit, then vote. A harder word helps too, because the two who know it can't prove themselves with the first thing that comes to mind. The Imposter words guide explains what makes a word easy or hard.

Fix 3: let anyone go first, imposter included

It feels kind to spare the imposter from speaking first with nothing to go on. Don't. If the first speaker can never be the imposter, everyone knows the starter is safe. With three players, the other person who knows the word can then rule out the starter and is left with exactly one suspect, so the round is over before anyone has said a word. Pick the starter at random from all three. The dealer on this site does that for you.

Fix 4: leave the imposter's hint on

In a three-player game, a caught imposter's last guess at the word is their only way back. A one-word hint keeps that guess alive. It is true but wide: if the word is Lawnmower, the hint might be Garden. The imposter now has something to bluff from, and the other two can't relax just because they have spotted the bluffer.

Here is how that plays out. The word is Lawnmower, and Ben is the imposter with the hint Garden. Amy goes first: "Noisy". Ben says "Weekend", vague but plausible. Cara, last to speak, says "Petrol". Amy and Cara both point at Ben, so he is caught. Then he puts Garden, Noisy and Petrol together, guesses Lawnmower, and wins anyway.

The lesson for the two who know the word: the last clue is the dangerous one. "Petrol" convinced Amy, but it also finished the picture for Ben. "Neighbours" would have rung true for anyone who has been woken by a mower on a Sunday, and left Ben choosing between a lawnmower, a barbecue and a barking dog.

Fix 5: score every round

Playing for a single win makes three-player Imposter a coin toss. Keep score instead: the imposter earns points for surviving the vote and for guessing the word, and the other two earn a point each for pointing at the right person. The full scoring is in the three-player variation. Play to five, and a bad round becomes part of a comeback rather than the end of the game.

For a twist, the Antics app has Mischief mode: some rounds, nobody gets the secret. Everyone is dealt the imposter card, so all three of you bluff at each other, each convinced you are the only one. With three players that small doubt changes everything, because the two who think they know the word can never be sure one of the others is lying.

Four or five players: the small-group fixes

  • With four, a two-two tie in the vote is common. Use the tie-break from the rules: one more clue each from the two accused, then vote again.
  • With four or five, play two circuits of clues. One circuit is not enough to tell a bluff from a bad clue.
  • Try question mode instead of clues. In a small group everyone gets asked something directly, so nobody can hide.
  • Keep it to one imposter. Two imposters needs a big room to hide in, and the app only offers it from six players.

Question mode and the other variations are on the Imposter variations page.

When three is just too few

If it still isn't landing after a few rounds, switch game rather than forcing it. Would You Rather and Never Have I Ever work brilliantly with three, because nobody needs a crowd to hide in. There are more ideas in games to play with friends.

Imposter with 3 people: FAQ

Can you play Imposter with 3 people?

Yes. Deal on a phone, keep clues to one word and one circuit, leave the imposter's hint on, let anyone start, and score every round so a caught imposter can still win on the guess.

What is the minimum number of players for Imposter?

Three. Four or more is easier, because with three the vote can be over before it starts, but the changes above make three a proper game. The free Imposter dealer on this site and the Antics app both run from three to twelve players.

How do you deal Imposter with only three players?

Use a phone. Paper strips need someone to write the word, and with three players there is a one in three chance that person draws the imposter strip already knowing the answer. A phone dealer shows the word to two players and nothing to the third.

How many rounds should three players play?

Play to a score rather than a number of rounds, for example first to five points. Dealing is random, so somebody can be the imposter twice in a row, and that is part of the fun.
