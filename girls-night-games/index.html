What makes a girls' night game work

The best girls' night games run on stories, not performance. Nobody wants to be told to stand up and do interpretive dance at eight o'clock with a sheet mask on. What they want is a reason to finally tell the story about the situationship, and a card that makes asking for it feel like the game's fault rather than yours.

So the test is short. One phone, no setup, nothing to print, and rules simple enough to explain while somebody is still hunting for a plate. A pass-the-phone game passes it, because the only equipment is the phone already in your hand, and it works as well on the floor or at the kitchen island as it does on the sofa.

It also has to work for whoever isn't drinking tonight. Every penalty in Antics is a forfeit, never a drink, so the friend who's driving home plays exactly the same game as everyone else and nobody has to explain anything.

Start with Never Have I Ever

It needs no shared history and no confidence, which is why it opens the night. Read the card out. Everyone who has done it takes a forfeit, and anyone is allowed one follow-up question. That second rule is where the night actually happens: the card says "ghosted someone and then bumped into them", and the follow-up is "where, and what did you say?"

Most Likely To is where the arguments start

On three, everyone points. Whoever collects the most fingers does the forfeit, and pointing at yourself is allowed.

It works best with friends who know each other well, which is exactly what a girls' night is. You already have opinions about each other. The card just gives you permission to say them out loud, and the fun is watching one person collect every finger in the room and demand a recount.

Would You Rather, for after the food

Would You Rather is the slow game, which is why it belongs after the takeaway, when everyone has gone horizontal. Nobody has to perform anything. The fun is making each person defend their answer, and a good one will run for ten minutes on its own while somebody explains, at length, why the bank balance is obviously worse.

How to run the night

Arrivals and the takeaway: Never Have I Ever. Everyone is seated, it takes no effort, and it does the catching up for you while the food is on its way.

Once the food is on its way: Most Likely To, while everyone is still sitting up.

After the food: Would You Rather, from the horizontal position.

Late: Truth or Dare. Or hand the rest of the night to Antics mode and stop choosing.

And stop while it is good. Twenty minutes of a game, then let the conversation it started carry on without you. The best bit of a girls' night is usually the story somebody tells because of a card, not the card itself, and the phone will still be there when it goes quiet again.

Common questions

What games can you play on a girls' night in?

Pass-the-phone games are the easiest, because they need no props, no printing and no setup: Never Have I Ever, Most Likely To, Would You Rather and Truth or Dare. Antics has all four plus five more games, nine in all, on one phone, so whoever is hosting just passes theirs round.

What are good Never Have I Ever questions for a girls' night?

The ones about exes, group chats and nights out land best, because everyone has a story and nobody needs to know each other's history to play. Start with something gentle, like a message sent to the wrong group chat, then work up to situationships and walks of shame.

How do you play Most Likely To?

Read the card out, count down from three, and everyone points at the person they think fits. Whoever gets the most fingers takes a forfeit. Pointing at yourself is allowed, and it works best with friends who already know each other well.
