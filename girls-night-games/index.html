Girls' night games for a night in: 15 that actually work

The takeaway is ordered, someone has brought the good face masks, and the group chat that took three weeks to organise all this has finally gone quiet because everyone is in the same room. This is the point where a girls' night either turns into the best catch-up you have had in months or into five people scrolling their own phones on one sofa. A game tips it the right way.

Here are fifteen games for a grown-up girls' night in. Eight run in Antics, our party games app, and most of those also run free in your browser. The other seven are classics that need little more than a pen, a coin or a timer. Each one comes with how it works, what you need and the group size it suits, and six of the Antics games come with a few cards to try, copied straight from the app.

A few cards below name a player. In the app that is whoever you put in the game; on this page we have made up the names.

What makes a girls' night game work

The best girls' night games run on stories, not performance. Nobody wants to be told to stand up and do interpretive dance at eight o'clock with a sheet mask on. What they want is a reason to finally tell the story about the situationship, and a card that makes asking for it feel like the game's fault rather than yours.

So the test is short: no setup, nothing to print, and rules simple enough to explain while somebody is still hunting for a plate. Every game below passes it, and they all work as well on the floor or at the kitchen island as they do on the sofa.

It also has to work for whoever isn't drinking tonight. Every penalty in Antics is a forfeit, never a drink, so the friend who's driving home plays exactly the same game as everyone else and nobody has to explain anything.

Games on your phone

These eight are all in Antics, free to download on iPhone and Android. Pass the phone round, read the card out, and the app keeps track of everything else. No app yet? Six of them run as a free round in your browser, and Imposter and The Four Kings have browser versions of their own.

1. Never Have I Ever

Someone reads a confession out. Everyone who has done it owns up and takes a forfeit, and anyone is allowed one follow-up question. That second rule is where the night happens: the card is the setup, and "where, and what did you say?" is the story.

What you need Antics, or the free browser version.

Group size 3 or more. It needs no shared history, which is why it opens the night.

Five to start with, from the Antics deck. Never have I ever…

  1. Been messaged by an ex’s new partner.
  2. Fancied a mate’s ex and kept it to myself.
  3. Cried in a club toilet.
  4. Accidentally liked a crush’s photo from three years ago.
  5. Bumped into an ex while on a date.

The full rules and plenty more: Never Have I Ever questions.

2. Most Likely To

Read the card, count down from three, and everyone points at who fits it. Whoever collects the most fingers takes the forfeit. Pointing at yourself is allowed, and so is demanding a recount.

What you need Antics, or the free browser version.

Group size 4 or more, so the pointing means something. Best with friends who already have opinions about each other.

Five from the Antics deck. Who's most likely to…

  1. Leave someone on read but watch all their social media stories.
  2. Know the name of every ex their mates have ever had.
  3. Still be getting ready when the taxi’s outside.
  4. Get AI to write their breakup text.
  5. Have a screenshot ready for every argument.

More, with the rules: Most Likely To questions.

3. Would You Rather

Two options and no third choice. Everyone picks one, then has to defend it. It is the slow game, so it belongs after the food, when everyone has gone horizontal and a good one can run for ten minutes on its own.

What you need Antics, or the free browser version.

Group size 2 or more. It works at any size.

Four to argue about, from the Antics deck. Would you rather…

  1. Your mum joins the group chat, or the group chat gets dinner with your mum monthly?
  2. Get a handwritten apology from everyone who’s ever ghosted you, or have to write one to everyone you’ve ghosted?
  3. Unsend one message from your past, but everyone’s notified you did, or leave it out there forever?
  4. Know exactly what your ex tells people about you, or guarantee they say nothing at all, ever?

More: Would You Rather questions.

4. Truth or Dare

Pick truth or dare, then answer honestly or do the dare. In Antics, anyone who chickens out takes a forfeit instead. Save it for later in the evening: truths first, dares once the room is loud enough that nobody minds being watched.

What you need Antics, or the free browser version.

Group size 3 to 8. Much bigger and people wait too long for a turn.

Two truths from the Antics deck:

  1. What ick have YOU definitely given someone?
  2. What’s a text you’ve drafted and never sent?

And two dares that work on a sofa:

  1. Act out how Sam flirts.
  2. Recreate Priya’s most recent profile picture. They judge the accuracy.

5. Imposter

Everyone gets the same secret word except one player, the imposter, who only gets the category and a one-word hint. Go round the room giving one-word clues, then vote on who is bluffing. Too obvious a clue and the imposter works out the word; too vague and everyone suspects you.

What you need Antics, or play Imposter free in your browser.

Group size 3 to 12 in the app, best with 4 to 8. From six players you can add a second imposter.

Never played? How to play Imposter has the rules and an example round.

6. What Are The Odds

Ask someone what the odds are that they will do the dare on the card. They pick the odds, say 1 in 10. Count down from three and both shout a number between 1 and 10 at the same time. Same number, and it happens.

What you need Antics, or the free browser version.

Group size 2 or more. It is played in pairs, so it moves fast round any size of room.

Three from the Antics deck. What are the odds…

  1. You read the last text you received out loud like a dramatic soaps cliffhanger.
  2. You mime getting ready for a night out until someone guesses three products.
  3. You admit which player’s outfit you’d steal tonight.

The full rules: What Are The Odds.

Classics with no phone

For when the phones go in a bowl, or you just fancy something with no screen. None of these needs more than paper, a pen, a coin, a bowl or a timer.

9. Two Truths and a Lie

Each person says three things about themselves: two true, one made up. Everyone else guesses the lie. The best rounds are the true stories that sound invented. Antics mode deals it as a card too.

What you need Nothing.

Group size 3 or more.

10. Paranoia

Whisper a question to the person on your right, one whose answer is somebody in the room. They answer out loud with just a name. Then flip a coin: heads, the question gets told to the room; tails, it stays a secret, and the person named has to live with not knowing.

What you need A coin.

Group size 4 or more, sitting in a circle.

11. Hot Seat

One person takes the hot seat for two minutes while everyone else fires questions at them. They answer honestly, with one pass allowed. Then the next person sits down.

What you need A timer.

Group size 3 or more.

12. Song Association

Someone says a word. The next person has five seconds to sing a line of a song with that word in it. Blank, and you are out for the round; sing it, and you pick the next word for the person after you.

What you need Nothing.

Group size 3 or more, round in a circle.

13. Who Am I?

Write a famous name on a sticky note and stick it on the forehead of the person next to you without them seeing it. Take turns asking yes or no questions to work out who you are. First to get theirs wins.

What you need Sticky notes and a pen.

Group size 3 or more.

14. Charades

Act out a film, TV show, book or song title without speaking while your team guesses against the clock. Show the number of words on your fingers first, and tug your ear for "sounds like".

What you need Nothing, or slips of paper with titles on.

Group size 4 or more, in two teams.

15. The Hat Game

Also called Celebrity or Salad Bowl. Everyone writes a few famous names on slips of paper and drops them in a bowl. In two teams, take turns against a one-minute timer: in round one you describe the name without saying it, in round two you get one word, and in round three you act it out. The names stay the same every round, so it gets funnier as it goes.

What you need Paper, pens, a bowl and a timer.

Group size 4 or more, in two teams.

How to run the night

Arrivals and the takeaway: Never Have I Ever or Two Truths and a Lie. It takes no effort and does the catching up for you while the food is on its way.

Once the food is here: Most Likely To, while the room is still upright.

After the food: Would You Rather or the Hat Game, from the horizontal position.

Late: Truth or Dare, Paranoia or Imposter. Or hand the rest of the night to Antics mode and stop choosing.

And stop while it is good. Twenty minutes of a game, then let the conversation it started carry on without you. The best bit of a girls' night is usually the story somebody tells because of a card, not the card itself.

Common questions

What games can you play on a girls' night in?

The easiest are pass the phone games: Never Have I Ever, Most Likely To, Would You Rather, Truth or Dare, Imposter and What Are The Odds. With no phone at all, try Two Truths and a Lie, Paranoia, Hot Seat, Song Association, Who Am I?, Charades or the Hat Game. This page explains all fifteen.

What are good girls' night games for adults?

Never Have I Ever and Most Likely To, because they run on stories about exes, group chats and nights out, then Truth or Dare once the room has warmed up. Paranoia gets the biggest reaction: a whispered question, a name said out loud, and a coin flip deciding whether anyone finds out why.
