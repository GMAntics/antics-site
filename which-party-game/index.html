The short answer

If you only have ten minutes, pick by mood. To get to know each other: Never Have I Ever, or Would You Rather once there are five or more of you. For chaos and laughs: What Are The Odds, or Most Likely To from nine. For deduction and bluffing: Imposter from three players, or What Are The Odds with two. For confessions: Never Have I Ever. With an hour or all night you get two games in the order to play them, and the opener can change: the tables below have every answer.

None of it needs equipment. One phone gets passed round the room, and every game here is in the Antics app, with most of them free to play in your browser on this site.

How the picker decides

The picks follow five rules, all taken from how the games actually play.

  1. Headcount comes first. Never Have I Ever, Truth or Dare and What Are The Odds all work with two, so the picker keeps two of you to those. The other games are listed for three or more: Would You Rather, Antics mode and The Four Kings can be adapted for two, but Most Likely To with two is just pointing at each other. Imposter needs three, and the app deals it for up to twelve.
  2. Small groups want cards that land on everyone. With three or four, games where every card asks something of each person get sharper, and pointing games need four or five before they find their feet. More on that in party games for small groups.
  3. Strangers get the gentle openers. Never Have I Ever has the lowest barrier to entry of any party game and works with strangers, and Would You Rather lets everyone answer at once, so nobody is put on the spot. Most Likely To is saved for groups with history, so it never comes up for getting to know each other.
  4. Ten minutes means one game. Something you can start in seconds and stop at any card. Never The Four Kings, which keeps going until the fourth King comes out.
  5. A long night is built, not picked. An hour gets two games back to back. All night gets two to build around, switching at the first lull. Spicy, the 18+ deck that comes with Premium, only ever comes in late, never as the opener.

Which party game: questions

What party game should we play?

It depends on how many of you there are and what kind of night you want. With two, What Are The Odds, Truth or Dare and Never Have I Ever all work. With three or four, Never Have I Ever and Would You Rather land on everyone. With five to eight, Imposter and Most Likely To are at their best. With nine or more, Would You Rather and Most Likely To scale best because everyone votes at once.

What is the best party game for two people?

What Are The Odds, because every card is a duel between two people anyway: one asks, the other sets the odds, then you both shout a number. Truth or Dare and Never Have I Ever work with two as well. Imposter needs at least three players.

What is the best party game for three people?

Would You Rather is a natural fit, because a vote between three people always has a minority unless everyone agrees. Never Have I Ever, Truth or Dare and What Are The Odds all play well with three, and Imposter works from three if you keep the hint on.

What party game works for a big group?

Would You Rather and Most Likely To scale best for big groups because everyone votes at once. Imposter works too: the Antics app deals it for up to twelve players on one phone, with a second imposter from six, and past ten it is worth splitting into two circles. More than twelve of you? Run two circles for every game.

What party game is good for people who don't know each other?

Never Have I Ever has the lowest barrier to entry of any party game and works with strangers, and Would You Rather lets everyone answer at once so nobody is put on the spot. Save Most Likely To for your actual mates.

Do we need anything to play?

No. Every game here runs on one phone passed round the room. Most of them play free in your browser on this site, and all nine are in the Antics app, which is free to download on iPhone and Android, with no ads and no sign-up.
