The football Imposter game: words and hints

This is the football deck, the one your five-a-side group wanted after getting humbled on synchronised swimming in the Sports deck. It is the game as Britain actually lives it, from Panini stickers in the playground to the Boxing Day match, and the imposter has to bluff every bit of it.

Football works for Imposter because nobody in a British group starts from zero. Even the mate who claims to hate it knows the Match of the Day tune and has had a Half-time Pie take the roof of their mouth off. The deck leans on that: less tactics, more of the stuff around the game, so the non-fan can hold their own and the fan who knows everything still has to find a clue that proves it without handing the word over. VAR is a lovely opener, because everyone has an opinion and none of them are calm. Nutmeg is the one that catches the crew: it means a ball through someone's legs, and the mate who clues "Cinnamon" is about to be voted out for knowing the word and not the game. Every word is in the list below, with both of its hints.

How to run a football round

  1. Open Imposter in Antics and pick Football in the lobby. Play it on its own, or select Sports as well for a longer night.
  2. Set the hints. Easy suits a room with a non-fan in it, because the easy hint is usually an object or a person that goes with the word. Hard is for the five-a-side lot: the hint is a mood or a moment, and it gives far less away.
  3. Pass the phone round and everyone peeks.
  4. No clubs and no players in your clues. One mention of your team and the round turns into an argument about last weekend, which is exactly the cover an imposter wants.
  5. No singing, no chanting, no humming. Humming the Match of the Day tune is the same as saying it.
  6. Vote. A caught imposter gets one guess, and it has to be a proper answer: "some sort of foul" gets a laugh, not the win.

The hardest football words to bluff

The hardest words are the ones that are not things. Offside is a rule half the room has never managed to explain, and its easy hint points two ways, because Corner Flag is in the deck too: an imposter holding "Flag" is on a coin toss before anyone has spoken. Away End does the same with one word, since a non-fan reads "Coach" as the manager rather than the bus full of travelling fans and spends the round in the dugout. Parking the Bus is the cruellest for anyone who does not watch: it is a tactic, not a place or an object, and the fans clue it in a language the imposter does not speak. Watch the words that sit next to each other as well, like Referee and Final Whistle, or the Half-time Pie and the Half-time Oranges. The app deals the imposter a hint each round. Easy then hard: Offside: Flag / Confusing. Away End: Coach / Outnumbered. Parking the Bus: Deep / Boring.

Who the Football deck suits

Your five-a-side WhatsApp group, obviously, because it settles who actually knows the game and who has been nodding along since school. It fills the half hour in the pub before kick-off while you wait for the teams to come out. It is a good shout on a stag do, where the groom's mates and his dad's mates finally have something to talk about. And it is brilliant at a grown-up family Christmas with one non-fan at the table. They are the most dangerous imposter in the room, because everyone expects their clues to be vague, and Boxing Day Match is in the deck, which feels about right.

Can you play football Imposter if some of you don't follow football?

Yes. Plenty of the deck is football as people live it rather than the game itself: the Scarf, the Half-time Pie, Panini Stickers, Jumpers for Goalposts. Keep the hints on Easy and the non-fans have something to work with when the phone makes them the imposter.

Are there footballers or clubs in the football deck?

No. It is the game and its rituals: VAR, the Away End, the Magic Sponge, the Knee Slide. Nobody's club decides the round, and Messi and Ronaldo live in the Famous People deck instead.

How is this different from the Sports deck?

Sports covers the whole sporting world, from darts to curling, and it is where football fans come unstuck. Football goes deep on one game, so the fans are finally on home turf. Penalty Shootout, Hat Trick and the World Cup already live in Sports, so they are not repeated here, and you can select both decks in the lobby at once.
