Christmas suits Imposter because everyone has the same memories and nobody tells them the same way. Secret Santa is the mug from whoever clearly drew your name at the last minute. Brussels Sprouts is the argument that comes round every December. Re-gifting is the bath set you are fairly sure started out as yours. It works at the office Christmas party once the speeches are done, on Christmas Eve when the telly has nothing on, and in the pub on the 23rd when everyone is back in town and the catching up has run dry. Every word is below, with both of its hints.

How to play Christmas Imposter

  1. Pick a word and write it on folded strips, one per player, with IMPOSTER and the hint on one strip. Whoever writes them is the dealer: they sit this round out and play the next.
  2. Everyone draws a strip and reads it in secret, then you go round the circle giving one clue each. No miming: a "ho ho ho" is the same as saying Santa.
  3. Vote on three. A caught imposter gets one guess at the word, and a right guess still wins it.

Tie-breaks, example rounds and what to do when someone blurts out the word are on the Imposter rules page.

Easy hint or hard hint: picking for your room

The easy hint is something you could point at. Tinsel gets Strands, Mince Pie gets Lid, Snow Globe gets Dome. Give the easy one for a first game, a big office crowd that has never played, or anyone who has been at the party since lunch.

The hard hint is the mood or the moment around the word. Santa gets Milk, Stocking gets Satsuma, Leftovers gets Grazing. It gives far less away, so save it for people who have played before and want the imposter to work for it.

Watch the sets, because that is where the hard hint bites. Christmas Tree, Tinsel and Baubles all hang off the same branch. Turkey, Brussels Sprouts, Pigs in Blankets and Leftovers all end up on the same plate, so an imposter who hears "gravy" has four answers and one guess, and Turkey's hard hint, Dry, could be any of them. Christmas Film, Christmas Songs and Christmas Card are close enough that a lazy clue fits all three.

Can you play Christmas Imposter at the office party?

Yes, and it suits an office party better than most games. Nobody needs to know anyone well, every word is common ground, and a round takes a few minutes. Keep Secret Santa in the pile: it lands differently when the person who bought your present is in the circle.

Should the imposter get the easy or the hard hint?

Easy for a first game or a big mixed group, because the imposter needs something to hold on to before the first clue. Once everyone has the hang of it, switch to Hard. Mistletoe's hard hint is Excuse, and that one starts a conversation on its own.

Why are there New Year words in a Christmas deck?

Because the deck is Christmas and New Year. New Year's Eve, Fireworks and New Year's Resolution keep it going in the odd days after Boxing Day, when nobody knows what day it is and the leftovers are nearly gone.

How many players do you need?

Four to eight is the sweet spot, plus whoever writes the strips. Three works with a few tweaks, covered in how to play Imposter with 3 people.
