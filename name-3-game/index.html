Name 3: three things, five seconds

Read the category. Start the clock. Name three things before it hits zero. Easy on your own, impossible with the whole room watching you.

How to play

  1. Whoever holds the phone reads the category out loud: Pizza toppings, say, or Things you find down the back of a sofa.
  2. They start the clock. Five seconds. Name three things in the category before it runs out.
  3. The room decides. Three fair answers in time is Got it. A blank, a repeat or a clock at zero is Failed, and Failed means a forfeit. Then the phone moves on.

That is the whole game. It works with two people and with twelve, needs no setup and no pen, and one round takes about ten seconds, so it slots in between anything else you are playing.

House rules that keep it fair

What counts as an answer?

One word, a name or a short noun. "Cheese" counts. "That one with the red bits" does not. If the room has to argue about it for longer than the round took, it is a fail.

Can you repeat an answer someone else gave?

Yes, unless your group says otherwise. Banning repeats makes the popular categories brutal by the third lap, which some groups love.

Five seconds is too short

In the app the clock is yours to set, anywhere from three seconds to ten. Start on five, drop to four once everyone is warmed up, and give a newcomer a little more if you like.

Heckling

Allowed. Encouraged, really. Counting down out loud at the person on the spot is half the fun, and the reason nobody can name three farm animals with the room shouting.

What makes a good category

The best ones sound easy. Everybody knows colours, so when your mind goes white on Colours the whole room enjoys it. A strong category has dozens of right answers, and none of them come to you under pressure.

  • Things in a fridge
  • Farm animals
  • Countries beginning with S
  • Famous people called David
  • Worst places for a first date

The app deals categories without repeats until you have seen the lot, and some of them name someone from your group, so the room gets a say in what counts.

Questions people ask

Is Name 3 the same as the five second game?

It is the same idea: a category, a short clock and three answers. Name 3 is how the Antics app plays it, with its own categories and a forfeit when you fail.

How many people do you need?

Two or more. It plays best with four to eight, passing one phone round the circle.

Is Name 3 free?

Yes. Name 3 comes with free categories in the Antics app, and Antics Premium unlocks the full deck along with every other game.

Is it a drinking game?

No. A fail means a forfeit, so everyone can play whatever is in their glass.
