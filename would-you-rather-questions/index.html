Party rules: read a dilemma out loud. On three, everyone points left or right (or shouts A or B). The minority takes the forfeit. Ties mean everyone takes it, obviously.

The real rule: you must choose. "Neither" costs a double. The follow-up interrogation of whoever voted weird is the actual game.

This guide explains the traditional game as it is played offline. The Antics app, for iPhone and Android, is a party-only app built on forfeits and contains no drinking instructions.

Making it a forfeit game

The voting mechanic is what turns a car-journey game into a party game: everyone commits publicly, the minority pays, and the room immediately demands justifications. Offline it is often played with drinks; the better stake is a forfeit, and we keep 40+ forfeit ideas for exactly this. Same arguments, no hangover.

Would You Rather FAQ

How do you play Would You Rather as a party game?

Read a dilemma, count down from three, and everyone votes at once: point, shout, or hands up for option A. Whichever side has fewer votes takes the forfeit. Refusing to choose costs a double.

What if the vote is a tie?

Everyone takes the forfeit. It's the only fair outcome and the game gets better if ties feel dangerous.

Can you play Would You Rather sober?

Completely: the minority takes a forfeit. The game is the choices and the arguments.

Play it right: keep the forfeits silly, give everyone a veto, and look after your mates.
