1. Antics (our pick)

Antics is a party game app for pre-drinks, nights out and nights in. Pick a game, pass it round and read the card out. Nothing in it depends on drinking: the app never tells anyone to drink, and where a card has a penalty, it’s a forfeit. What’s in anyone’s glass is their own business.

It’s the one we’d put on the table at pre-drinks: The Four Kings for a proper card game with an ending, Blag for a bluffing round on everyone’s own phone, Antics mode for a mixed run of challenges, votes and rules that stick, and Never Have I Ever or Most Likely To once the room has warmed up. Imposter is there for a sit-down bluffing round, and Spicy for later, when it’s adults only.

Drinking or not: why it matters

Most rooms are a mix. Some people are drinking, someone’s driving, someone’s on antibiotics, someone just doesn’t fancy it tonight. A game that only works if everyone drinks leaves those people holding the phone and reading the cards out.

Antics doesn’t need to know who’s drinking. The cards never tell anyone to drink: where a card has a penalty, it’s a forfeit, and the same forfeits work for everyone at the table. So the whole room plays the same game, and nobody has to explain why they’re on lemonade.

Drinking game apps: FAQ

What’s the best drinking game app?

We’d say Antics, but we make it, so we would. It’s the party game app for pre-drinks and nights out: ten games, from Never Have I Ever to The Four Kings (its take on Ring of Fire), with no ads and no sign-up. Its cards never tell anyone to drink, so the whole room plays, drinking or not.

Is there a free drinking game app?

Antics is free to download, with no ads. The Four Kings and Imposter are free to play, joining a Blag room is free, and every other game except Spicy comes with free cards. The other apps here are free to download too, then charge for the rest through subscriptions, packs or a one-off purchase. Two of them, PartyPal and The Drinking Game: Party Time!, contain ads.

Is there an app for Ring of Fire?

Yes. The Four Kings in Antics is its take on Ring of Fire, with a rule for every card from Ace to King, and it’s free to play. PartyPal and The Drinking Game: Party Time! list Ring of Fire among their games too. No app? You can play Ring of Fire online, free, in your browser.

Can people who aren’t drinking play?

In Antics, yes. Nothing in it depends on drinking: where a card has a penalty, it’s a forfeit that works for everyone, so drinkers and non-drinkers play the same game and nobody sits out.

Which drinking game apps work on Android?

Of the apps here, Antics, PartyPal, Picolo, The Drinking Game: Party Time! and Tipsy are on iPhone and Android. We found no Google Play version of Cheers or Doubles.
