ANTICS

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 September 2026

TL;DR: Antics has no accounts, no ads and no tracking across other apps or websites. We never know who you are. The app makes a random anonymous ID on your phone and sends it with usage statistics that identify nobody; purchases are keyed to an anonymous customer ID held by our purchase provider (in some versions the same ID). Neither identifies you.

What we don't collect

Antics has no user accounts, no sign-up, no advertising and no tracking across other apps or websites. We do not collect your name, email address, contacts or location. What you answer and who you play with is never recorded: player names you type in never leave your phone.

What the app stores on your phone

To make the app work between sessions, Antics saves a small amount of information in your phone's local storage:

  • Your local gameplay progress (like which cards you have already seen)
  • Any player names you type in
  • A random anonymous ID, created once, the first time you open version 1.1.0 or later. It is not tied to your name, Apple ID, email or anything else about who you are, and cannot be reversed to reveal anything about you
  • A count of how many times you have opened the app, and the date you first opened it. Both are sent with the anonymous usage statistics as plain counts (for example, "opened 12 times, installed 30 days ago"), keyed to your anonymous ID

Any player names never leave your phone, and neither does the progress file itself (the usage statistics below do record each prompt card shown, by number). The anonymous ID and the two counts are sent to our own servers with the usage statistics described below. Everything in this list is removed from your phone when you delete the app; copies already sent are covered under How long we keep data. Reinstalling the app creates a new anonymous ID, unless your phone restores the app's data from a backup. Restoring a purchase afterwards can let our purchase provider connect the new install to the purchase records of your previous one (see Purchases, below).

Purchases

Payments are handled entirely by Apple or Google Play, whichever store you installed from: we never see your payment details. To unlock what you've bought (and restore it if you reinstall), the app uses RevenueCat, a purchase-management service. RevenueCat holds an anonymous customer ID for this install and your purchase history for this app. Depending on the app version, that customer ID is either the same anonymous ID described above or a separate random ID that RevenueCat generates. Either way it is tied to this install's purchases, never to a person: not to your name, Apple ID, email or anything else about who you are, and it is not used for advertising or shared with anyone else. If you restore a purchase after reinstalling, RevenueCat's restore process moves your purchase records to the new install's customer ID so your purchase keeps working. Where purchases and usage statistics share one ID, that restore also lets us connect your old install's usage history to the new one; it never links either to a person. See the RevenueCat privacy policy.

Referral codes

If you use the invite feature, our server generates a referral code linked to the first anonymous ID our purchase provider gave this install (this stays separate from the on-device ID described above, even in app versions where purchases otherwise share that ID, and is never a name) and stores it, along with which codes have been redeemed, so that rewards can be granted fairly. Redeeming happens in Apple's own offer-code sheet; Apple's report tells us which code was redeemed, when and in which country, never by whom. We never receive any identifier for your phone itself (no hardware or advertising IDs), and none of this referral data can be traced back to you personally.

Anonymous usage statistics

To understand which games get played and improve the app, Antics records anonymous events such as "a game was opened" or "a card was shown", with counts such as session length, how many times the app has been opened, and how long ago it was installed. Our lawful reason for this is legitimate interest: we need to see how the app is used to improve it. We do not advertise to you or build a profile of you. Each event also carries the app version, build number, operating system version, platform and which store the app was installed from; on Android, the first launch also records which campaign link (if any) the app was installed from. These events carry the anonymous ID stored on your phone, so we can tell how a single install behaves over time, for example whether people tend to come back the day after installing. Where that ID is also our purchase provider's customer ID for the install (see Purchases), we can also see whether an install kept playing after buying something. This never reveals who you are: there is no name, email or contact information attached, no one, us included, can turn it into a person or a phone, and it is never used to advertise to you anywhere. We record which card was shown and for how long, never names, typed text or what anyone said: the statistics describe how the app is used, never a private conversation. The anonymous ID on your phone is the User ID on our App Store privacy label, which is why the label lists usage statistics (and, through our purchase provider's ID, purchases) as linked to you: linked to your install, never to a person.

Antics also uses Aptabase, a privacy-first analytics service, which receives the same events without your anonymous ID. Aptabase keeps its own separate session that is not linked to that ID, and we do not join the two.

Third parties

The complete list of services involved in running Antics:

  • Apple: payments and offer-code redemption
  • Google: payments on Android
  • RevenueCat: purchase unlocking and restore, keyed to an anonymous customer ID for this install
  • Supabase: hosts the referral-code database (keyed to the purchase provider's first anonymous ID for the install, see Referral codes) and our own copy of anonymous usage events (keyed to the anonymous ID on your phone)
  • Aptabase: a second, separate copy of anonymous usage statistics, not linked to your anonymous ID
  • Cloudflare: cookie-free visitor counts for this website only

None of them receive your name or anything else about who you are. There is no advertising software in the app at all.

How long we keep data

We have no automatic deletion schedule yet: anonymous usage events and purchase history stay on our servers until you ask us to delete yours. Referral codes and redemption records are kept for as long as the invite feature exists. Aptabase keeps its copy under its own retention rules (see its privacy policy). Deleting the app removes the ID from your phone; it does not delete the usage events or purchase records already on our servers. Contact us (below) if you would like us to locate and delete your existing records as well.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask what data is held, ask for it to be deleted, and object to or ask us to restrict this processing. Because everything is keyed to an anonymous ID rather than your name, we may not always be able to tell which records are yours from an email alone, so please include as much detail as you can (roughly when you installed, what you bought, and on which platform). Deleting the app removes everything stored on your phone, the ID included; on a reinstall you get a new anonymous ID unless your phone restores the app's data from a backup. Emailing us lets us attempt to clear any server-side records tied to the old one too. If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

Age requirement

Antics is designed for adults aged 18 and over and carries a corresponding App Store age rating. It contains mature, suggestive and sexual party-game prompts. Some prompts include infrequent references to alcohol, but the app does not instruct or encourage alcohol consumption.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Antics changes how data is handled, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be clearly described here.
