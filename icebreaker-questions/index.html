A good icebreaker question is easy to answer, takes under a minute and gets a laugh without putting anyone on the spot. Every question below comes from the clean deck behind the free Antics generators: nothing about relationships or bodies, nothing a manager would wince at, and anyone can pass.

They come in four shapes. A Would you rather makes everyone pick a side, which is the gentlest start because nobody has to say anything personal. A Never have I ever gets people owning up to small, shared embarrassments. A plain question asks for a short story. A Who is most likely to gets the room pointing at each other, so save it for people who have met. Mix the shapes and the room warms up faster, and when a question is not enough there are one minute activities too.

Icebreaker questions for work

Work icebreakers have one job: get everyone to say one thing out loud early, so the meeting that follows is a conversation and not a lecture. Keep them light, keep them quick, and answer first yourself so nobody feels tested. Three situations come up again and again: the regular team meeting, a new starter's first week, and the remote call where half the cameras are off.

TEAM MEETINGS Would you rather…

Read one out, everyone answers on three, then ask one person from each side to defend their pick. Two minutes, start to finish.

  1. Be the best player on a losing team, or the worst on a winning one?
  2. Get one extra hour every day, or one extra day every week?
  3. Have a photographic memory, or perfect instincts?
  4. Be brilliant at something nobody values, or average at something everybody needs?
  5. Have your work praised by strangers, or quietly by the person you admire most?
  6. Wear the same outfit every day, or eat the same lunch every day?
  7. Always be ten minutes early, or always be ten minutes late?
  8. Start every conversation with a compliment, or end every one with a joke?
  9. Be remembered for one small kindness, or one enormous achievement?

TEAM MEETINGS Questions to go round

One question, everyone answers in a sentence or two, the person running the meeting goes first. Nothing here asks for anything private.

  1. What is the worst advice you have ever actually taken?
  2. What small thing annoys you far more than it reasonably should?
  3. What song do you know every word to and wish you did not?
  4. What is the worst present you have ever received?

TEAM MEETINGS Who is most likely to…

For a team that already knows each other. Everyone points on three, and whoever gets the most fingers explains themselves. Nothing here stings, which matters when the whole team is in the room.

  1. Build a spreadsheet for something that badly does not need one.
  2. Be the calmest person in a crisis.
  3. Bring snacks nobody asked for and save the entire day.
  4. Have a secret talent nobody here knows about.
  5. Read the terms and conditions properly.
  6. Volunteer for something before hearing what it is.
  7. Be running the place in ten years.
  8. Keep a receipt from years ago for no reason at all.
  9. Send a group message that was meant for one person.

A NEW STARTER Questions for everyone

The whole team answers, not just the new person, so it feels like a welcome rather than an interview. One or two a day across the first week beats ten in one go.

  1. What did you want to be at seven years old, and what happened?
  2. What is the most useless skill you are genuinely proud of?
  3. What are you weirdly competitive about?
  4. What food combination do you love that everybody else finds odd?
  5. What is the smallest thing that has ever made your whole day?
  6. What is the best compliment you have ever been given?

A NEW STARTER Never have I ever…

Hands up if you have done it. These are the small mistakes everybody makes in a new place, so the new starter finds out quickly that the team has made them too.

  1. Forgotten someone's name halfway through introducing them.
  2. Nodded along to directions I did not follow in the slightest.
  3. Quietly googled something mid-conversation to pretend I already knew it.
  4. Photographed a whiteboard and never looked at the photo again.
  5. Called a teacher or a boss "mum" or "dad" by accident.
  6. Rehearsed a phone call out loud before making it.
  7. Pushed a door that clearly said pull, twice, in front of witnesses.

A REMOTE CALL Would you rather…

Paste one into the chat and everyone types 1 or 2 at the same moment, so the quiet people answer without having to unmute. Then ask one person from each side why.

  1. Have a phone battery that never dies, or a phone that never breaks?
  2. Live somewhere it is always summer, or somewhere it is always autumn?
  3. Have a personal chef, or a personal driver?
  4. Give up your phone for a month, or give up hot showers for a month?
  5. Have unlimited books, or unlimited films?

A REMOTE CALL Never have I ever…

Everyone who has done it raises a hand on camera or drops an emoji in the chat. Every one of these has happened to someone on this call.

  1. Talked for a full minute on a call before realising I was on mute.
  2. Turned my camera off in a meeting purely so I could eat.
  3. Been the last person to realise a meeting had ended.
  4. Hit reply all on an email that was meant for one person.
  5. Written something on a to-do list that I had already done.

ANY ROOM Quick icebreaker activities

When a question is not enough, give one volunteer a minute to do something. Ask, never pick, and the room claps whatever happens. All of these work on a video call too.

  1. Teach the group something useful in sixty seconds.
  2. Give a thirty second speech praising an object in this room.
  3. Talk for one minute on a subject the group picks, with no pauses.
  4. Describe the contents of your fridge as if it were a restaurant menu.
  5. Say the alphabet backwards as fast as you possibly can.
  6. Compliment every person here, one by one, and mean it.

Icebreaker questions for a new group

A new class, a seminar group, a club's first session, a corridor of freshers: a room full of people who have never met and would quite like to. Here the questions can be a little more personal, because everyone is in the same position. Start with a Would You Rather, move to a question each, and save Never Have I Ever for when people have stopped checking their phones. If it is freshers week, our guide to freshers icebreakers that aren't cringe has five games built for a new flat.

A NEW GROUP Questions to go round

One question, everyone answers, thirty seconds each. Easy to answer, hard to answer badly.

  1. Who was your first ever best friend, and where are they now?
  2. What is something you believed for far too long?
  3. What is your most irrational fear?
  4. What is the worst haircut you have ever had?
  5. What was the biggest lie you told as a child?

A NEW GROUP Never have I ever…

Hands up if you have. A room of strangers finds out fast that they have all done the same daft things.

  1. Sat in the wrong seat and stayed there out of pure embarrassment.
  2. Waved back at someone who was waving at the person behind me.
  3. Said "you too" when a waiter told me to enjoy my meal.
  4. Discovered I had been saying a word wrong my entire life.
  5. Laughed at a joke I did not understand even slightly.
  6. Started clapping at completely the wrong moment.
  7. Set eight alarms and comprehensively ignored every one.

A NEW GROUP Would you rather…

The safest opener there is: nobody has to share anything, and the arguments start on their own.

  1. Be able to talk to animals, or speak every human language fluently?
  2. Never feel tired again, or never feel hungry again?
  3. Have unlimited free flights, or never queue for anything again?
  4. Teleport once a day, or read minds for one hour a week?
  5. Meet everyone who lived before you, or everyone who comes after?
  6. Be the funniest person in the room, or the most interesting?

Icebreaker questions for friends who think they know each other

Old friends do not need the ice breaking. They need surprising, which is harder. The trick is to guess first: before anyone answers, the person on their left says what they think the answer will be. A right guess scores a point, and you find out very quickly who has not been listening for the last ten years. When the evening is long and it is definitely friends rather than colleagues, our full Would You Rather questions go a lot further.

FRIENDS Who is most likely to…

Everyone points on three. The person with the most fingers gets ten seconds to argue their case.

  1. Become extremely successful and tell nobody.
  2. Give brilliant advice and take none of it.
  3. Move abroad with two weeks' notice.
  4. Greet a celebrity thinking they went to school together.
  5. Take a brand new hobby far too seriously within a month.
  6. Forget their own birthday.
  7. Panic first in an emergency, then be the most useful.
  8. Survive the longest in the wild.
  9. Name a pet something ridiculous and commit to it forever.
  10. Do something kind and never mention it.
  11. Become famous for something completely accidental.

FRIENDS Questions to guess first

Guess the answer for the person on your left, then let them tell you how wrong you were.

  1. Who in this room would you want beside you in a crisis, and why?
  2. What is the nicest thing somebody in this room has done for you?
  3. What do you pretend to enjoy and secretly do not?
  4. What is the pettiest reason you have ever fallen out with someone?
  5. What is the most embarrassing thing your family has done in public?
  6. What rule do you break constantly and feel no guilt about?
  7. What is a talent you completely faked your way through once?

FRIENDS Never have I ever…

Before hands go up, everyone guesses who has done it. The ones nobody guessed are the best ones.

  1. Blamed a pet or a sibling for something that was entirely me.
  2. Hidden from someone I knew in a supermarket aisle.
  3. Pretended my phone was ringing to escape a conversation.
  4. Been thanked for something somebody else did and said nothing.
  5. Won an argument several hours later, in the shower, alone.
  6. Kept a houseplant alive for less than a fortnight.
  7. Assembled furniture and had parts confidently left over.

FRIENDS Would you rather…

Three that only work with people you trust, because the follow-up questions get personal.

  1. Always have to tell the truth, or always be believed?
  2. Be able to undo one thing you said, or hear one thing somebody never said?
  3. Always say exactly what you think, or never be able to?

How to run an icebreaker, with timings

The question matters less than how you run it. An icebreaker that drags for twenty minutes does more harm than none at all, so pick the length before you start and stick to it.

How long to give it, by room
RoomTimeHow to run it
Team meeting5 minutesOne Would You Rather for the whole room. Everyone answers on three, then one person from each side explains.
New starter5 minutes a day, first weekOne or two questions a day. Everyone answers, and the new person goes last, once they have heard a few.
Remote call5 minutesPaste a question into the chat, everyone types 1 or 2 at once, then call on people by name in a set order.
Any room, an activity1 minute eachVolunteers only. One person, one activity, then a round of applause, and pass it on.
New class or club15 minutesPairs first, two minutes each on one question, then everyone introduces their partner with their answer.
FriendsAs long as it is funGuess first, score a point for each right guess, and stop when the stories take over.

Five rules that stop it being awkward

  1. Go first yourself. People copy the length and tone of the first answer, so make yours short and a little silly.
  2. Anyone can pass. No reason needed. A pass costs nothing; a forced answer costs the room.
  3. Set a time limit. Thirty seconds each in a group of ten, a minute each in a group of four.
  4. Stop while it is going well. Two questions that land beat six that drag.
  5. Pick the question before the meeting. Scrolling for one live kills the moment, which is what the generator at the top of this page is for.

Icebreaker questions: FAQ

What are good icebreaker questions for work?

Questions anyone can answer in under a minute without sharing anything personal. A Would You Rather is the safest start, such as one extra hour every day or one extra day every week. Questions like the most useless skill you are proud of, or what you wanted to be at seven, work well too. Keep away from relationships, health and politics, and let anyone pass.

How long should an icebreaker take?

About five minutes at the start of a meeting, or fifteen for a new group that will spend the day together. Give each person around thirty seconds, go first yourself, and stop while people are still enjoying it.

What is a good icebreaker for a remote or video call?

A Would You Rather posted in the chat, where everyone types 1 or 2 at the same moment. Nobody has to unmute to join in, and you can then ask one person from each side to explain. Never Have I Ever works too, with a raised hand on camera for yes.

How do you do an icebreaker with friends who already know each other?

Make everyone guess first. Before a person answers, the player on their left predicts what they will say, and a right guess scores a point. Old friends are usually far worse at it than they expect, which is the fun.
