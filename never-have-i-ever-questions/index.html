This guide explains the traditional game as it is played offline. Antics for iOS is a party-only app built on forfeits and contains no drinking instructions.

How to play: someone says "Never have I ever…" and finishes the sentence. Everyone who has done the thing takes a forfeit (or drops a finger: pick your currency). Go around the circle taking turns. That's the whole game.

Finger version: everyone holds up 5 fingers (3 for a faster game). Done the thing = drop a finger. First to zero loses and takes the forfeit.

The one real rule: if nobody drops a finger, the statement was too safe. The follow-up questions are the actual game.

Playing it with forfeits

Offline, Never Have I Ever is often played with drinks. It does not need them: anything with a cost works, a forfeit, a point lost, a finger dropped. The game is the confessions, which is also why it survives family Christmas in a way Ring of Fire never will.

Never Have I Ever FAQ

What are the rules of Never Have I Ever?

Players take turns saying "Never have I ever…" followed by something they have never done. Anyone in the group who has done it takes a forfeit (or drops a finger in the finger version). There's no board, no cards, no winning: the game is the reactions and the follow-up questions.

Do you take the forfeit if you HAVE done it or if you haven't?

You take it if you have done it. The statement-sayer is safe by definition: they've never done the thing. (Saying something you secretly have done, just to trap the group, is a house-rule crime punishable by a double forfeit.)

How does the finger version work?

Everyone holds up five fingers (three for a fast round). Each time you've done the thing said, drop a finger. First player to drop all their fingers loses the round and takes the forfeit.

Is Never Have I Ever fine to play sober?

Completely: play it for forfeits or points. Of all the classic party games it needs drinks the least, because the content is the confessions.

Play it right: read the room: "in this room" questions are the best and the most dangerous ones. Look after your mates.
