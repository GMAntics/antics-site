9pm is party o’clock

The busiest hour to start a party is 9pm to 10pm, UK time: 15% of all parties kick off in that one hour. Three in five (59%) start between 6pm and midnight, and more start between 9pm and midnight (35%) than in the whole stretch from 5am to 6pm (28%).

A party starts at its first card that wasn’t skipped. Times are UK clock time. Most Antics groups play in the UK and Ireland, so a group abroad shows up at the wrong hour: an evening in New York lands after midnight here.

Saturday is the big night

A Saturday night gets 1.8 times as many parties as a Monday to Thursday night. Sunday and Friday come next. We count a night from 5am to 5am, so a party that starts at 1am on Sunday belongs to Saturday night.

Three in four parties play more than one game

75% of parties switch game at least once, and a third (32%) play four or more. It isn’t just the free cards running out: in parties with the full decks unlocked, two in three (67%) still switch.

Never Have I Ever and Antics mode each turn up in almost half of all parties (46% and 45%). Antics mode is the most common first game (27% of parties), and the most common switch is from Antics mode straight into Never Have I Ever (11%).

Truth goes first. Dare catches up

64% of Truth or Dare games open with a truth. Over every pick, dare edges it: 52% of all picks were dares. The longer a game runs, the braver it gets. In games that ran past 20 picks, the share of dares climbs from 40% in the first five picks to 49% after the twentieth.

A game here is one session of the app that played Truth or Dare, and every pick is a tap on the Truth or the Dare button. In the app, Truth sits above Dare, which may nudge the first tap. For long games each game counts equally, so one marathon game can’t outvote the rest.

One in seven Never Have I Ever cards gets skipped

About one in seven Never Have I Ever cards (15%) gets flicked past in under two seconds, nearly three times the rate for Truth or Dare (5%). Only Spicy, the app’s 18+ deck, is skipped more often (16%).

Would You Rather and Truth or Dare hold the room longest, at about 15 seconds a card. That’s more than twice as long as a Never Have I Ever card (7 seconds). Part of that is the format: one is a single line, the other starts an argument.

A skip is a card on screen for under two seconds: too quick to read out. A run of five or more quick flicks in a row counts as scrolling, not skipping, and is left out. Imposter deals whole rounds, not cards, so it isn’t in these charts. How we counted

Window
26 August to 4 October 2026, UK days, read on 5 October 2026.
Source
Antics’ own anonymous in-app analytics, from App Store and Google Play versions only. Phones on TestFlight, Google Play internal testing or a development build, and anything played in a web browser, are left out. The app never records names, typed text or anyone’s answers.
A party
One session of the app with at least 10 cards read. If a group closes the app and opens it again, that counts as two parties.
A skip
A card on screen for under two seconds. Runs of five or more quick flicks in a row are scrolling and are left out.
Small numbers
Every figure clears a minimum sample size, and anything thinner was cut.
Who this is
Antics groups, not everyone. Only groups who opened Antics are in it, and the app is rated 18+. Most play in the UK and Ireland.
What it can’t tell you
Who is in the room, how many people (adding names is optional), anyone’s age, or which language a group plays in. The app records only taps on its language setting, and there were too few to say anything.
